Teaching a 0.6B decoder to make Jev-style decisions on a 16 GB Mac

Four research questions, answered on one Apple M4 over five days: which fine-tuning recipe works, whether full fine-tuning beats an adapter, whether a thinking model helps, and whether an autoregressive LLM can be distilled into a one-pass decision model.

Answers at a glance

What we found

RQ1 · Which fine-tuning recipe is best for a decision model?
Stability first

Keep updates small and run two seeds; the rest is second order.

With 4,000 questions, 5 of 15 LoRA runs failed: learning rate 2e-4 on both seeds, rank 64 with alpha scaled to rank, and 2 of the 12 sane configurations by chance. Use lr ≤ 1e-4, fixed LoRA alpha, and reject runs whose dev log-loss sits at the uniform value. Rank above 16 and Brier loss do not help; 1,000 questions is too few.

Ablation details
RQ2 · Full fine-tuning or an adapter?
Adapter, on cost

Same accuracy; the adapter is 40x smaller to train and 50x smaller to ship.

Two seeds each: 0.815 vs 0.814 in domain, and the adapter leans about 2 points ahead out of domain, inside seed noise. The adapter trains 10.6M weights in 4 GB and ships as 43 MB. With very little data, full fine-tuning is the safer pick.

Two-seed comparison
RQ3 · Base Qwen or thinking Qwen?
Not worth it here

Thinking adds 3 points (not significant) at 95x the latency.

Qwen3-4B-Thinking reasons for about 1,100 tokens and scores 0.780 vs 0.750 for the instruct model (p = 0.41). It wins on rule-following records and loses on QNLI. As the student's starting point, the post-trained model ends up identical to the base.

Thinking vs direct answers
RQ4 · Can an autoregressive model be distilled into a decision model?
Yes

It transfers calibration reliably; extra out-of-domain accuracy does not hold up.

Half teacher, half gold labels matches gold-label accuracy and comes out calibrated with no correction (temperature 0.84–0.95 vs about 2.0). Teacher only works without any gold labels but costs 5.6 points in domain for a +1.7-point out-of-domain lean that is not significant.

Distillation results

Read every comparison against this: with the same recipe, changing only the training seed moved new-source accuracy by 3.8 points (adapter) and 4.6 points (teacher-only). That is larger than most method differences here, so gaps under about 4 points on one seed are treated as unproven. Paired tests on the same questions cover test-set sampling, not training randomness.

Setup

What a decision model is, and how every run was measured

A decision model reads a state (a ticket, a document, a review) plus typed questions, and returns a probability for each option in one forward pass, with no generated text. TypeSafe's Jev is the closed reference. tino attaches a pointer head to a decoder: each option's closing delimiter token is scored against a decide token, the same layout as the open Kev models.

RQ1 · Recipe ablation

Which fine-tuning recipe is best for a decision model?

One change at a time from a LoRA baseline, on a fixed 4,000-question subset for 2 epochs (about an hour per run), judged on development splits. Collapsed or stalled runs were rerun with another seed to separate a real effect from bad luck.

New-source development accuracy per configuration

trained normallycollapsed to uniformstalled at uniformdashed line: baseline mean of two seeds

A collapsed model gives every option the same score (spread under 0.002), so its dev log-loss is exactly 1.478, the uniform value. That fixed number is a cheap automatic check for a failed run.

  • Stability is the first-order effect at small data. lr 2e-4 collapsed on both seeds. Rank 64 with alpha scaled to rank collapsed; with alpha fixed at 32 it trains. Attention-only and teacher weight 0.5 each failed on one seed and trained fine on the other. No LoRA run collapsed at full data (0 of 7).
  • Smaller updates help a little. lr 5e-5 (0.750 / 0.593) and rank 4 (0.755 / 0.565) beat the baseline (0.729 / 0.559 mean) in domain. Each is one seed, so these are leads.
  • Attention-only adapters may transfer better. When it trained, it scored 0.606 on new sources, the best gold-label 4k run. One successful seed.
  • More rank does not help, and Brier loss hurts (about 5 points in domain, no calibration gain after temperature scaling).
  • Teacher soft labels raised transfer at 4k (teacher only: 0.611), but at full data that gain shrank to a non-significant 1.7 points (RQ4).
  • 1,000 questions is below the floor: both methods stayed near uniform in about 185 steps.
RQ2 · Full fine-tune vs adapter

Same accuracy, very different cost

Accuracy vs training data (development splits)

full fine-tuneadaptersolid: trained sources · dashed: new sources

At 4,000 questions the full fine-tune is ahead by 1.5–2 points; at full data the two converge. At 1,000 neither leaves the uniform plateau. Full-data points are two-seed means.

Cost per model

The full fine-tune's first seed peaked at 7.7 GB before a bug fix (the loss was read before the gradients, which kept every activation alive); its second seed, after the fix, peaked at 6.5 GB.

  • In domain the two are identical: 0.815 vs 0.814 over two seeds each.
  • Out of domain the adapter averages about 2 points higher on both new-source test sets, but its own two seeds differ by 3.8 points. A consistent lean, not a proven win.
  • The full fine-tune is more overconfident (fitted temperature 2.45–2.75 vs about 2.0).
  • Answer: use the adapter. Switch to full fine-tuning only when data is scarce.

  • 3a: over all 200 questions thinking scores 0.780 vs 0.750 (95% interval −3.0 to +9.0, McNemar p = 0.41), at 31.6 s vs 0.33 s per question. 43 of 200 traces hit the 2,048-token cap, so thinking may be slightly understated.
  • 3b: post-trained vs base start, same adapter recipe: 0.810 vs 0.810 trained sources, 0.615 vs 0.613 new sources, 0.532 vs 0.548 hardest (all p ≥ 0.18).
  • Answer: for millisecond decisions, answer directly; reserve thinking for rule-heavy cases that can afford 30 seconds. The student's starting checkpoint does not matter.
RQ4 · Distillation

Distilling an autoregressive teacher into a one-pass decision model

The teacher is Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 at 4 bits. Each question becomes a lettered multiple-choice prompt, and the next-token probabilities of the letters, softened by a temperature of 9.0 fitted on the calibration split, become soft labels for all 14,576 trainable questions. The student is the adapter recipe with loss α·KL(teacher ‖ student) + (1−α)·cross-entropy on gold labels.

Fitted temperature per run

full fine-tunegold-label adapterdistilled

A temperature near 1.0 means the raw probabilities are already calibrated. Every distilled run lands at 0.84–0.95; every gold-label run needs 1.96–2.75 to remove overconfidence.

Half teacher vs gold labels: identical accuracy everywhere (sign tests p ≥ 0.44), with better out-of-domain Brier on both seeds.

  • Half teacher, half gold is the recommended model. It matches gold-label accuracy, needs no temperature correction, and has out-of-domain ECE of 0.04–0.05 against 0.11–0.15 for the gold-label adapter.
  • Gold labels are not required. Teacher only averages 0.758 in domain and 0.649 out of domain, 87% of the teacher's out-of-domain accuracy in a model a sixth the size, at about 50 ms per question instead of 330.
  • Teacher only is a trade, not a win. It loses 5.6 points in domain on both seeds. Its first seed's 0.671 out of domain looked like a breakthrough; the second seed scored 0.626.
  • What carries over is calibration; the teacher's broader knowledge mostly does not fit into a 0.6B student, matching Kev's finding that capacity limits this size out of domain.
Released models

Five models on Hugging Face

All private under BrandeisPatrick. Each Hub copy was downloaded and re-scored, and reproduces its run's test accuracy exactly. Bar set at the start: trained ≥ 0.808 and new ≥ 0.642 (Kev-0.6B), calibrated ECE ≤ 0.05, ≤ 8 GB to train, ≤ 100 ms per question.

No released model clears both accuracy bars. A second-seed adapter (not released) scored 0.818 / 0.651 and cleared both, which again shows how much seed luck sits in single numbers. Pick tino-distil by default.

Engineering log

What broke, and what fixed it

  1. Full fine-tuning with AdamW peaked at 10.1 GB and left the Mac 19–24% free on every step. Switching the backbone to Adafactor removed about 3.5 GB of optimizer state.
  2. The first adapter run spiked to 9.2 GB. Reading the loss before computing gradients ran the forward pass alone and kept every activation alive, defeating gradient checkpointing. Evaluating loss and gradients together brought training to 4.0 GB with identical results.
  3. Casting the fp32-trained model to bf16 flipped 18% of decisions on a smoke model, so every model is evaluated and published in the precision it was trained in.
  4. The memory guard stopped a 2.6-hour teacher labeling run after a check ran alongside it and swap grew 3.6 GB. Six of seven splits were already uploaded; the labeling resumed and every job since ran alone.
  5. A queue waited on itself: its wait loop matched its own parent shell's command line. Fixed in five minutes by waiting on a process ID.
  6. LoRA collapse was found from the logs, not guessed: collapsed runs had frozen dev log-loss at 1.478 and option scores within 0.002 of each other. Seed reruns then separated real failures (lr 2e-4) from chance ones.
  7. A second seed reversed a headline. Teacher-only distillation looked like the best out-of-domain model at 0.671; the rerun scored 0.626, and the pooled gain is not significant.
Limits and next steps

What these results do not settle

  • Most ablations have one seed; the seed reruns show single runs can swing several points out of domain.
  • The thinking comparison used 200 questions and a 2,048-token reasoning cap.
  • Distilling from the thinking model would need reasoning traces for all 15,576 training questions, about 60 hours on the M4; it needs a cloud GPU.
  • Worth testing next: attention-only adapters with several seeds, lr 5e-5 at full data, and a larger teacher such as Qwen3.5-9B for labels.
