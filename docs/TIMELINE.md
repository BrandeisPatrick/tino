## Day by day

### Tuesday, October 21: framing

The question was whether this laptop could turn a decoder into a decision model "like Jev" three ways: plain fine-tuning, adapter layers, and distillation, with every run tracked off the machine. The inventory found an M4 with 16 GB, MLX installed, no PyTorch, and GitHub already logged in. A search identified Jev as TypeSafe's typed decision model (choice, score, yes/no in, calibrated probabilities out, one forward pass) and found six open clones, of which Kev became the reference: same base, same data, a published LoRA recipe. The verdict was feasible at the 0.6B scale, with ten follow-on ideas.

### Wednesday, October 22: harness and first model

The plan page went up, and the goal was fixed as three models named `tino-finetuned`, `tino-adapter` and `tino-distil`. The repository was created at 22:39 with the MLX harness: Kev's input layout and pointer head, pinned data with checksums, and a tracker that refuses to train from uncommitted code and pushes every run at every evaluation. Patrick stopped the first smoke run before it started, asking that the machine never be pushed to a crash. That produced the memory guard (22:44). The next smoke run peaked at 10.1 GB under AdamW and left the Mac at 19–24% free memory; switching the backbone to Adafactor brought it to 7.5 GB (22:46). A check showed casting the trained model to bf16 flipped 18% of its decisions, so models are published in fp32 (22:51). `tino-finetuned` started at 22:51 and ran for 183 minutes overnight.

### Thursday, October 23: adapter, teacher, queue

At 01:55 `tino-finetuned` finished at 0.810 on trained sources and 0.618 on new ones, was published, and was re-downloaded and re-scored. LoRA support went in next. The adapter smoke run peaked at 9.2 GB for 10.6M trainable weights; the cause was reading the loss before the gradients, which kept every activation alive. Evaluating them together brought training to 4.0 GB (08:52). `tino-adapter` ran 08:56–12:09 and matched the full fine-tune exactly in domain.

Patrick then set four research questions: the best recipe, fine-tune versus adapter, base versus thinking Qwen, and whether an autoregressive model can be distilled into a decision model. Those became `RESEARCH.md`. Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 was chosen as teacher after a probe against Qwen3.5-9B. Labeling started at 13:10 and was stopped by the guard at six of seven splits after a distillation check ran alongside it and swap grew 3.6 GB; it resumed at 14:05 and finished at 15:32. From 15:36 a single queue ran everything one job at a time: `tino-distil` (to 18:53), the thinking comparison (to 20:40), and `tino-adapter-post` (to 23:59).

### Friday, October 24: ablations and seeds

From midnight the queue ran thirteen ablations at 1,000 and 4,000 questions. By mid-morning the logs showed five runs with dev log-loss frozen at 1.478, the uniform-guess value, and option scores within 0.002 of each other: the collapse fingerprint. A follow-up queue was written to rerun each failed setting with a second seed and to test rank 64 with a fixed alpha. `tino-adapter` seed 1 (11:05–14:27) scored 3.8 points higher out of domain than seed 0, which set the rule that single-seed gaps under about 4 points are unproven. `tino-finetuned` seed 1 ran 14:27–17:35, then the follow-ups from 17:36, ending with the full-data teacher-only model `tino-distil-a1` (22:23–01:43), whose first seed scored 0.671 on new sources.

### Saturday, October 25: second seeds and write-ups

`tino-distil` seed 1 ran 01:43–05:05, confirming that distillation keeps gold-label accuracy and comes out calibrated. The findings page was published and the final RQ1 and RQ4 answers written. Patrick asked for a second seed of `tino-distil-a1`; it ran 08:36–11:49 and scored 0.626, reversing the out-of-domain headline. RQ4 was corrected: calibration transfers reliably, extra out-of-domain accuracy does not, and `tino-distil` became the recommended model. The lessons page followed.

### Monday, October 27: organization

Patrick asked whether all the lessons were in the repository (three of nine were not) and how to organize it so every detail is tracked. The answer was `LESSONS.md`, a generated run index and model registry, `docs/TRACKING.md`, `docs/ENGINEERING.md`, the three pages saved under `docs/pages/`, the collector script, and this timeline.

### Wednesday, October 28: public repository and the article draft

The repository went public in the evening, after a scan of all 402 tracked files found no tokens, keys or personal addresses (commits carry the GitHub no-reply address; the model weights stay on private Hub repos). Then the article draft, asked for as "less text, more graph": `scripts/figures.py` renders nine figures from `docs/results.json`, the per-question prediction files and the zero-shot evaluation, including a paired bootstrap interval and sign test for every comparison behind the four questions, and `scripts/build_article.py` turns `paper/article.md` into a page with the figures inlined and recoloured for light and dark themes. The text stays short and the captions carry the numbers. Drawing the figures sharpened two points: pooled over two seeds, the adapter's out-of-domain lean over full fine-tuning is +1.9 points on both new-source splits (p = 0.02 on the hardest), still smaller than the gap between the adapter's own seeds; and on new sources the gold-label students, when about 85% confident, are right only 50 to 60% of the time, where the distilled students track the diagonal.

### Thursday, October 29: Phase 2 begins

Patrick asked for explorations that make the four answers "academically acceptable": more sizes, more tests, more seeds. The answer was `docs/PHASE2.md`: what Phase 1 cannot claim, six hypotheses written down before any run, and three tiers of experiments priced in hours, about five days of compute. A 1.7B adapter smoke run measured the cost of the size step (4.9 GB, 2.3 times slower). Patrick chose Tiers 1 to 3 without the 1.7B full fine-tune and the 4B adapter. The afternoon built the tooling: a file-driven queue (`scripts/queue_run.sh`) so jobs can be added while it runs, a port of Kev-0.6B's PEFT adapter and pointer head into the tino format (`scripts/kev_port.py`), and `scripts/eval_suites.py`, which scores models on suites no run was selected on and runs two probes. The queue started at 20:59. Its first job was stopped at 21:48 by the guard's swap rule, which other processes had tripped; the limit went from 3 to 6 GB and the job was re-queued. The suite evaluation ran 21:48–23:07: the Kev port reproduced its card within a point (0.809 and 0.631 against 0.801 and 0.620), and ten models got their first numbers on five never-seen suites. One defect surfaced: the SemIf perturbation groups came out empty because perturbed rows point at their base through `parent_id`, not the group id; the metric was fixed and the run rescored from its saved predictions.
